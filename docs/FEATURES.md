# Prison Escape 3D: what the game can do

A high-level map of every feature in the game, and of what the code could support beyond it. It
is meant as the starting point for deciding what to build next, including gameplay that has
nothing to do with prisons.

Numbers are as of **v2.24.0**, read from the content modules. For the rules of a single feature
go to [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md); for the original design, the

## Platforms

- **Runs in the browser**, on desktop, phone and tablet, with no download and no account.
- **Installable app (PWA):** a service worker stores the whole game, so an installed copy plays
  offline and offers updates (an update downloads only the files that changed). The title screen's
  Install button guides each browser.
- **A quick start:** a loading screen with a prison joke shows at once while the game downloads;
  French, the tutorial, the manhunt and the side games' cards come in their own files. A game's
  start and the way back to the title show a short loading veil while the prison is built.
- **Screen shapes:** every screen and moment of play checked at two desktops, four phone sizes (upright
  and turned) and a tablet both ways, in English and French. A phone turned in the browser goes full
  screen at the first tap in a game (its bars took a third of the height).
- **Performance:** three graphics tiers. On Auto, phones start one tier lower, and the game steps
  down when play averages under 40 fps. `?perf` in the address shows a frame counter (frames a
  second, a frame's time, the graphics card's, the tier, what's drawn), kept on until `?perf=0`; or
  the switch in the settings' Developer section.
- **Sharing:** a Share button with a QR code, copy link and the phone's share sheet.
- **Fully local:** saves, records and settings stay on the device. The game has no server.
- **Comfort settings:** reduced motion, camera shake, vibration, sound and music volume, amount of
  speech bubbles, warden announcements, first-time tips, holidays, graphics, language.
- **Accessibility:** the interface's size on a phone or tablet (100, 115 or 130%: the HUD, cards and
  buttons grow together, as far as the screen has room; on a desktop the browser's zoom does it, on an
  iPhone or iPad Safari's page zoom), and
  the main actions' keys changed one by one (the Keys card, a key taken swapping with the other
  action; every text and prompt names the player's keys).

## Controls

Keyboard, mouse, touch, a gamepad and test bots all produce the same packed command, so a new control
scheme never touches the game's rules.

|---|---|---|---|

| Walk to a spot | Click the spot | Tap the spot | — |
| Act (search, pick up, open, talk, sit, sleep) | E | Action button | A |
| Use the tool in hand, hit | Space (hold to repeat) | Tool button (hold), red hit button | X (hold to repeat) |
| Lock on to someone | R, or click them | Lock button, or tap them | Y |
| Block (held) | V | Shield button, in a fight | LT |
| Dig up out of a tunnel | U (hold) | Dig up button (hold) | R3 (hold) |
| Hold an item | 1–6 | Tap a pocket | LB, RB |
| Drop the item in hand | Q | From the pockets panel | L3 |
| Throw | Hold right mouse to aim, or F straight ahead | Tap the item, Throw, drag to aim, lift | RT straight ahead; held, aim with the right stick and let go |
| Craft | C | Hammer button | View, Crafting |
| Your file, the day's card | I, J | Portrait, clock | View, Your file / The day |
| Rat, phone, crew, odd job | T, P, G, B | Their pills on the HUD | View, then the panel |
| Lesson objectives, How to play | O, H | Bulb button, from the pause card | View, then Objectives / How to play |
| Zoom | Mouse wheel | Pinch | Right stick |
| Close a panel, pause | Esc | Pause button | B closes, Menu pauses |

Every key in this table but the digits, Esc and the arrows can be changed (Settings, Keys); the
activities below keep theirs, except walking left and right. On an AZERTY keyboard the keys are read by position (Z Q S D) and shown with their real labels. A
page going out of sight pauses and saves.

**A gamepad (2.45)** plays the whole game, menus included: any standard pad (Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro,
8BitDo and most others). Its layout is fixed, by the buttons' places (the Xbox names above); the hints name them
as the pad says (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo or 8BitDo letters), or as picked in Settings, Gamepad buttons, for a pad
that passes for another (an 8BitDo in X-input mode). On a card or a screen the D-pad moves a highlight between the buttons, A presses and B goes
back; where a card's game is played with the arrows (the gym, arm-wrestling, penalties, the stage show)
the D-pad and stick are the arrows. View opens a wheel of the panels, the pockets among them (an item's
menu: Wear, Throw). Once the pad is used, every hint, lesson and How to play names its buttons, until a
key or a finger is used again. It rumbles where a phone vibrates.

## Core mechanics

### The day

The bell runs everyone's life. Being where the routine says, for long enough to be counted, keeps
suspicion down; the routine is also the player's cover.

| 08:00 | 09:00 | 10:00 | 12:00 | 15:00 | 16:00 | 17:00 | 18:00 | 22:00 | 23:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roll call | Breakfast | Free time | Work | Exercise | Showers | Dinner | Free time | Roll call | Lights out |

Free time is the player's own: its banner and the day's card say what it opens (the merchants, games with inmates,
the pay phone, the kiosk, the sports field, the garden). From the second day the crowd gets together: rings chatting,
pairs at dice or cards, two shooting hoops. Walk up to someone at a game and the action button joins it; stand by a
chat and you overhear something true. At 21:50 a warning, and the floor arrows, send the player to roll call.
Play an inmate at arm-wrestling, Cheat, hoops or checkers and those nearby with nothing better to do come over to
watch: they cheer, groan and shout, and win a bout or a shootout in front of them and each thinks a little more of you.

### Heat

Heat (0–100%) is how suspicious the guards are, and it cools on its own out of sight, twice as fast
where the routine wants the player (even under a guard's eye). It rises for
skipping the routine, showing contraband, entering restricted zones and searching other inmates'
cells. At 80% guards chase on sight. Caught, the player loses carried contraband and wakes in the
infirmary, or in solitary from 90%, where peeling potatoes shortens the stay. Lockdowns, cell
searches at 08:00 and bed checks at 00:30 and 04:00 complete the pressure. A bed dummy fools the
night checks, tucked into the bed by the action button even after Lights Out. The walk home has until 23:25, a warning coming at 22:50, and then the cell block's lights go out.

### Your prisoner

- **Three attributes:** Strength, Speed and Intellect. They are trained at weights, pull-up bars and mats,
  treadmills, punching bags and laps, and books, pressing in time with a beat, and slip back when neglected.
  Inmates use the machines too, and give way when asked for a turn.
- **Energy and health:** tools and training cost energy. Sitting, meals, showers, chocolate and sleep
  restore it; at night only sleep does, past 20%, so a night out has a cost the next day. A meal is a tray taken at the serving counter, eaten at a table and carried back (eaten and not brought back, it costs heat); what it gives is the day's dish, on the menu board. A cup at the water cooler gives a little energy now and then, and cools a heatwave's heat. At roll call you stand on your own square and answer when the warden calls. A shower is turned on with the action button: energy back and minor heat off while it runs, the inmates taking turns.
- **Pockets:** 6 hotbar slots, a weapon slot and an outfit slot. Desks have a hidden compartment that
  morning searches never find.

### Stealth and perception

- **Sight:** each guard has a 7-tile vision cone, blocked by walls and shut doors, and shown on screen
  in yellow (patrol), orange (investigating) or red (chasing). After dark it reaches 4 tiles, further
  only where it's lit (lamps, rooms with their lights on), and the cone shows where it stops.
- **Noticing:** a guard takes a moment to be sure of what it sees (a "?" filling over it, quicker close
  up); out of its sight first, and it only comes to look.
- **Hearing:** tools and thrown objects make noise that spreads along walkable tiles. Walls stop it
  and doors muffle it, so throwing something is a working distraction.
- **Guard character:** every guard is named and is steady, lazy (naps on night rounds), strict (longer
  searches, reports bribes) or corrupt (takes bribes).
- **Dogs:** smell contraband, follow the player's trail and can be bought off with treats.
- **Disguise:** a guard's uniform works until a guard gets close for too long.

### A world in layers

Each map has four layers with cutaway views: **tunnels**, **ground**, **vents** and **roofs**.

- **Destructible:** pickaxes chip walls, cutters cut fences, screwdrivers open vents, shovels dig.
- **Tunnels:** need timber braces every 2 tiles or they cave in. Dirt is evidence, flushed down
  toilets or left below ground.
- **Cover-ups:** posters, fake walls, fake vents and fake fences hide damage from guards who would
  otherwise raise the alarm.
- **Doors and keys:** five colours of key door. Keys are stolen or cast from a mold, and doors can be
  picked with a timing mini-game.

### Combat

Lock on and strike (an inmate who hits you is locked on at once), or hold the block for half the damage.
Damage depends on Strength, weapon and outfit. The knocked out stay down for 30
seconds and can be searched. Inmates hold grudges, and knocking out about 85% of the guards at once
is itself a way out (the takeover).

### Security systems

| System | How to beat it |
|---|---|
| Generator and power grid | Switch it off for 45 seconds without fence, cameras, detectors and searchlights |
| Electric fences | Cut the power first |
| Cameras | Tape or toothpaste over the lens, for a minute |
| Detector arches | Carry contraband in a pouch |
| Searchlights and snipers | Time your crossing; a storm grounds the snipers |
| Mines | Tunnel under them, or take the supply road |
| The sea | Tunnels flood: leave by raft, helicopter or the drawbridge |

## People and money

- **Living inmates:** they follow the routine, chat and hold opinions of the player (0–100) that
  change with gifts, favors and how they are treated.
- **Speech:** bubbles with moods and voices. Gossip is always true, generated from the running game,
  and useful tips are kept in the player's file.
- **The crew:** at opinion 80, recruit up to two followers: lookout, digger, muscle or smuggler. They
  can stay up for the night of the escape and come along.
- **Gangs:** the Kitchen Crew, the Lifers and the Gym Rats, in their colours, each holding a canteen
  table and a corner of the yard. Join through an initiation for cheaper goods and backup in a fight;
  rivals take it badly, their turf is off limits, and once a week two gangs go to war.
- **Jobs:** laundry, janitor, kitchen, woodshop, mailman, metalshop, gardener; at the Six Hunter, kennel hand, smokehouse and net mender. Each has a station loop,
  an Intellect requirement and a daily quota. They pay $30–65, and missing the quota gets you fired.
  Inmates work theirs too, and guards mind you in a work room that isn't yours.
- **Merchants:** prices follow opinion. One merchant in each prison is the fixer, who sells the burner
  phone.
- **Odd jobs:** a cork board with three jobs a day from 12 kinds (lift, drop, frame-up, fixed game,
  rat mail and more), paid in cash, rare items or debts owed. Each prison has a three-step story.
- **Smuggling:** order on the burner phone, delivered by night drone or by a visitor.
- **Pay phone:** calls home, a lawyer, anonymous tips, and Cousin Lou, who arranges diversions, a cake
  with a file in it or a getaway car. Guards listen in, so the player chooses plain or coded phrases.
- **Internet kiosk:** mail, a legal shop, news that forecasts coming days, and courses for Intellect.
- **Vending machine:** in every canteen, snacks and drinks for energy or health and toiletries for crafting,
  paid for and dropped there and then. Its rows run low as inmates queue for it, now and then something
  sticks and has to be thumped free, and it's off in a lockdown.
- **Menu board and water cooler:** the board shows the day's breakfast and dinner, a different dish every day,
  each with its own energy and some health, and tomorrow's; scribble on it for a laugh, if no guard is looking.
  Inmates chat at the water cooler in free time: stand by while two are there and you overhear a rumour.
- **The pet rat:** trap and tame it, then send it to fetch or plant small items up to 16 tiles away.

## Days that differ

Each day's plan is worked out from the prison and seed, and the warden reads it out at 08:00.

- **Events (12):** inspection, fire drill, feast, generator service, movie night, food fight, delivery,
  visiting, sports day, talent night, quiz night, gang war. Each changes the rules for a few hours and opens
  its own chances.
- **Weather:** fog, rain, storm, heatwave and wind, with snow and blizzards in the cold. Weather changes
  how far guards see and hear, the power, sniper fire, gardens, the glider and hoops.
- **Holidays, by the real date or picked in settings:** Easter (egg hunt), summer (water fight),
  Halloween (costume night) and Christmas and New Year (presents, fireworks), each with decorations,
  items and trophies.
- **Twists (21, in the Daily Escape):** rule changes such as Eagle Eyes, Closed Shop, Bare Hands, Lone
  Wolf, Big Freeze or a three-day limit. Each has a difficulty cost, and the generator keeps at least
  one way out possible.

## The manhunt

After going over the wall, the escape already counts, and the player can choose to keep running for a
**clean getaway star** on the medal. Each prison has its own outside map (60×56 tiles).

- **Against you:** searchers with vision cones and radios, tracking dogs, roadblocks, a helicopter with
  a searchlight, and a search that widens over time.
- **For you:** hedges and culverts to hide in, fords that wash away your trail, washing lines with other
  clothes, stones to throw as distractions, dog treats, cutters, Lou's car and a crew member who
  whistles.
- **The start depends on the escape:** over the wall, out of the gate, off a glider, from a van, ashore
  from a raft or from a helicopter. Legal releases (paperwork, parole) have no manhunt.

## Progression

- **Unlocks:** each escape opens the next prison, and the first opens the Daily Escape.
- **Medals:** gold, silver or bronze per way out per prison, by the number of days taken.
- **Achievements (144):** ways out 21, speed 3, conduct 5, skills 33, prison life 34, days 10, Daily 7,
  holidays 5, mishaps 19, career 7. Many unlock cosmetics.
- **Your prisoner:** name plus 70 cosmetics (skin, hair, style, beard, build, hat, glasses, tattoo,
  trim), shown in-game and on a portrait.
- **Lifetime stats:** a headline and titled groups on the trophy shelf.
- **How to play:** 69 searchable topics, most with an in-game picture or diagram, key numbers, steps,
  recipes and a button to play its lesson.

## Sound and picture

- **3D view:** a fixed top-down orthographic camera with zoom, walls cut away around the player, day
  and night, shadows, and rain, snow, fog, lightning and particles.
- **Characters:** one skinned humanoid rig with 30+ activity poses and many looks, plus dogs, rats and
  animated vehicles (trucks, drone, helicopter, raft, glider, car).
- **Music and sound:** fully synthesized, with 4 music moods, ambience by scene, murmured voices,
  weather and dozens of effects.
- **Interface:** plain DOM screens over the 3D canvas, with design tokens and screen-reader labels.

## Today's limits

These matter most when planning gameplay beyond the prison.

| Limit | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Maps are 64×64 tiles with 4 layers** | Fixed constants that the world, paths and rendering rely on. A larger world is real work |
| **One fixed camera** | Orthographic, looking north, zoom only. No rotation, third-person or first-person view |
| **Flat, tile-based movement** | 16 directions on a grid, no physics engine or jumping. The ball and glider are special cases |
| **One player, no network** | No multiplayer, leaderboards or accounts. The Daily is the same for everyone because it is computed from the date |
| **`Sim` assumes a prison** | A map needs cells, roll call and guards. The routine, zone names and step order are built in |
| **No asset pipeline** | No loading of models, textures or audio files. Everything new is built in code |
| **Old runs must replay** | A rule change that alters logged runs needs a version flag, and new state fields go at the end |
| **Every text in two languages** | New text needs English and French, enforced by `i18ntest` |
| **Every feature carries upkeep** | Lessons, help topics, map viewer entries and pictures are part of a feature's definition here |

## Ways to extend

Three levels of effort, smallest first.

| Approach | Good for | How it fits | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New content or rules in the existing `Sim`** | New items, events, twists, holidays, mini-games, or a re-themed map that keeps the daily structure | Add data and a system, as phases P27–P42 did. Must start after day 1 or be opt-in so the escape bots stay identical | Low to medium |
| **A mini-game as its own mode** | Penalties, rhythm, cards or racing played on their own from the title screen | The logic already exists, but it reads inmates, opinion and money from the prison state, so it needs separating | Medium |
| **A separate simulation** | Gameplay with different goals on the same engine: exploration, survival, a heist, a chase, town life | Follow `src/sim/hunt/`: its own state and steps, reusing world, paths, sight, movement, commands and view. Old prison runs are untouched | Medium to high |

Anything needing a free camera, physics, bigger worlds or multiplayer would change the foundations, so
it is worth settling early whether a new idea depends on one of those.
